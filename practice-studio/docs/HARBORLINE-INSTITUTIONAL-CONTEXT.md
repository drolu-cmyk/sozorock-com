# Harborline Institutional Context

Harborline's present-day work should be explainable by its history, ownership and operating model.

## Institutional memory
The company has grown through analytics expansion, cross-border operations, cloud adoption, organizational change and internal AI adoption. Legacy conditions are consequences of that history, not random defects inserted for exercises.

## Clients and vendors
Named clients and selected vendors in the simulation are fictional. Real technology providers may appear where learners need authentic professional-system experience.

## Policies
Policies are versioned organizational artifacts with owners and effective dates. A learner may encounter a policy that does not perfectly match current technical reality. That mismatch can itself become legitimate work.

## Identity
Employee, contractor and service identities have different authoritative sources and lifecycle expectations. Nested groups and service identities create realistic IAM investigation paths.

## Atlas
Atlas is shared infrastructure across pathways:
- IAM can investigate human and service access;
- Applied AI Systems can inspect retrieval, authorization, evaluations and runtime behavior;
- GRC can test controls and evidence;
- AI Governance can examine inventory, classification, deployment gates and monitoring.

The same underlying fact should be reusable across roles rather than rewritten into separate course scenarios.
